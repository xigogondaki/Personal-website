Budget 2025: Signals for Canadian Agriculture and Agri-Food

February 15, 2026  ·  3 min read

The release of Budget 2025 earlier this month surfaces some important signals for the future of Canadian agriculture and agri-food. Sharing this policy brief by the Canadian Agri-Food Policy Network, which provides an insightful breakdown of what to expect.

The Budget identifies agriculture as a strategic sector. We are encouraged to see targeted, regional funding that supports early-stage agricultural innovation. Continued investment in areas like CEA and precision ag can serve as a catalyst for the kind of commercialization that Canada needs. A commitment at the federal level towards Buy Canadian procurement also has the potential to create strong and consistent demand for Canadian products and solutions.

Contrastingly, there is a reduction of up to 15% in spending by Agriculture and Agri-Food Canada. While it is still not clear where those cuts will fall, reductions in science and innovation spending could stall R&D and critical research, leading to long-term implications for innovation in agri-food. There is also no mention of new federal initiatives for CEA and early-stage ag-tech manufacturing.

Overall, the Budget looks to balance immediate stability from a farmer's perspective via risk-management support, while also trimming spending on science — signaling a trade-off between short-term resilience and long-term innovation. It will be interesting to see how Budget 2025 plays out in the coming years.
