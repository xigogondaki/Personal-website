Canada's AgTech Talent Gap: A Strategic Risk We Can't Ignore

January 16, 2026  ·  4 min read

Really interesting article by RBC that speaks to a growing trend in Canadian agricultural innovation. Less than 1% of STEM graduates are choosing careers in agriculture-related occupations. Public investment in Canadian agriculture has declined 15%, and R&D spending is down by 77%. Meanwhile, the U.S. agri-food industry continues to skyrocket, with up to 22x the investment we see in Canada.

This imbalance is startling, considering that we have a rich history of agricultural innovation and the second largest greenhouse industry globally. There is also a clear demand for fresh and home-grown produce as consumers wish to support the local economy. We are at a critical juncture and cannot allow innovation and talent in this sector to drain south. AgTech must be part of reorienting the Canadian economy toward self-sufficiency. A strong domestic food supply chain is as strategic as it gets, and investments in R&D, skill development and agri-food entrepreneurship are key.

Our climate puts indoor food production front and centre in the challenge of developing year-round resiliency, but the vast majority of Canadian fresh produce farms are unable to contribute at all in winter. This is not a lack of will, but a techno-economic gap.

Can Grow Here has been working hard to be a part of the solution. With over five years of R&D behind us, we've been focusing on empowering large numbers of small-to-medium scale farmers with high-tech, low-cost, 4-season infrastructure.

What do you think? What is needed to shrink the agri-food talent gap and reverse the decline in public and private investment? How can we harness Canada's highly educated workforce to invigorate this key industry?
